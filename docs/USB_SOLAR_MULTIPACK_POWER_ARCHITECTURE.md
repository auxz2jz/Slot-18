Recorded: 2026-10-07 America/Los_Angeles. STATUS: **REQUIREMENT CONFIRMED; CIRCUIT TOPOLOGY PLANNED, NOT BUILT OR TESTED**. ## Confirmed user operation
- **Swap only entire removable cartridges during device operation**. The electronics must keep the remote station or handheld powered with at least one other adequately charged cartridge OR with external power.
- Each remote station: **two removable cartridges, nominally four 18650 cells in each** (eight total), subject to final pack dimensions/current measurements. Main handheld: **two removable four-cell cartridges initially, potential expansion up to four cartridges** (8 to 16 cells), subject to weight/clearance and actual load.
- A detached cartridge is user-openable so individual 18650 cells can be inspected, replaced and charged after it has been taken out. It is *not* allowed to service bare individual cells while cartridge is inserted.
- Both handheld and remotes get an **external power/charging port** which should power the device directly and simultaneously charge installed packs *when the external source has enough capacity*. No internet needed.
- Both types support **optional solar panel input**, providing live system load first and using surplus PV energy to recharge cartridges if conditions permit.
- Automatic input preference: appropriately sized USB/wall adapter when available, then solar input where appropriate, then batteries when external power is unavailable or insufficient; do not inadvertently connect wall input and solar together without engineered input-selection/backfeed protection.
- Preserve **per-cartridge** SoC, actual measured voltage/current/temperature where instrumented, fault, charge/discharge/idle/active, aging/SoH where a capable gauge is fitted, and estimated minutes left (estimated, not guaranteed). Send each remote's status to main ESP32 and later Android/web; show one vs two present, and external USB/solar source active.
- Cartridge outer shell will be 3D-printed with recessed keyed, appropriately rated blind-mate/docking connector, mechanical retention and protected exposed contacts. Use one cartridge footprint when practical across remotes/handheld.

## Important design clarification: cells INSIDE cartridges
Option A (cost/size preference, evaluate professionally): matched 4-cell 1S4P pack in removable holder/cartridge with properly chosen protection, current/temperature monitoring and **one correctly sized 1S charger per cartridge**. The 4 cells share the same parallel voltage. A discharged new cell dropped into a partially charged parallel set creates potentially severe equalization current. To service, first remove cartridge, disconnect/power down, assess health/voltage/capacity of ALL cells, use matched cells; in practice **replace the matched set** if one is damaged or markedly degraded. Do not imply arbitrary one-cell replacement is safe just because cartridge is off-device.
Option B (higher cost but true independent per-cell service): four isolated/protected single-cell channels inside each cartridge, with per-cell charging/health supervision and engineered discharge OR paths. Individual cells may then be changed offline without hard-paralleling unequal voltages; validate inrush, one-channel power load, reverse current and thermal dissipation. This may be preferable if selective single-cell replacement is an important requirement, but adds hardware size/cost and needs a real electrical design.
Option C: 4S series pack plus 4S balancing BMS and 4S charger; lower external current but breaking the series pack is potentially risky and all cells need balancing/matching. Not selected by default. BQ25798 '1–4-cell' spec means up to **four SERIES cells** per charger, not four independent packs; cannot connect two/4 cartridges to one battery terminal and call it isolated charging.

A charger must never be tied directly across two unrelated removable cartridges; a single-channel charger for one multi-cell *pack* is not by itself a multi-pack hot-swap charger.
A properly selected charging/power-path IC may integrate some input/mux/load/charging functions; still must engineer the independent multiple pack channels, common 5V output and safe hot insertion/removal.
Input current management must reserve enough power for LOAD before enabling/scheduling battery charge, and fall back safely to battery if solar drops at dusk/shade or USB is unplugged. Charger should suspend/reduce current if panel/source cannot supply both. Partial clouds and low solar may produce zero surplus charging.

- **Adafruit BQ24074 USB/DC/Solar charger** example: for a *single 1S Li-ion/LiPo pack*, 5–10V input, power-path load sharing up to 1.5A; OUT varies around 3–4.4V, not regulated 5V; requires separate 5V conversion and per-pack separation. https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-bq24074-universal-usb-dc-solar-charger-breakout . Demonstration building block, NOT two-pack or 4-pack independent charger. It is not true MPPT, uses input voltage control to prevent solar panel voltage collapse.
- Potential independent pack load sharing: verified correctly rated power MUX / ideal-diode OR with MOSFETs, inrush/current limits, backfeed prevention; charging paths must remain independent and not be accidentally blocked by the discharge ideal diode.
- USB-C PD controller/negotiation may be required for charging multiple packs at appreciable rates: 5V/3A input is only ~15W total before losses, potentially insufficient for full-brightness display + radars + 2–4 chargers. A PD-capable **charger-side** 20V supply, when properly negotiated/rated, can provide more input power; never apply PD 9/12/20V directly to the Waveshare's 5V input.
- Use per-pack rated protection/BMS, fusing or current limit, thermal sensors, genuine current-rated recessed mating connectors; important hot-unplug under load transient is a hardware test, not a software feature.
- For external solar, use solar-specific MPPT / panel voltage regulation and properly selected PV voltage/current panel, not a bare panel directly to 18650s or a cheap standard TP4056 charging module unless panel regulated and battery topology protected. Panels and Li-ion cells should not share a sun-heated sealed container; battery must remain in safe charge temperature range.

## Example power budget and panel size — illustrative, NEVER promised runtime
Using 8 x quality 3000mAh 18650s at 3.6V nominal = 86.4Wh nominal. With 80% assumed effective usable energy -> about 69Wh; around 34h at average 2W station load, 28h at 2.5W. Actual measurement and converter/cell/BMS behavior controls.
For a continuously operating 2W remote station: daily load = 48Wh/day. A nominal 20W panel with an illustration of 4 equivalent full-sun hours/day and 70% net capture gives ~56Wh/day, leaving only ~8Wh/day for recharge; cloud, winter, shading and poor orientation can make actual capture much less. A **20–40W solar panel** may be reasonable to evaluate for a 1–2.5W station, but the true size needs site/season measured sun-hours and desired recovery rate. A nominal 30W panel under these same example assumptions gives ~84Wh/day, ~36Wh available to recharge after a 2W load; not guaranteed. Main handheld may average ~4–6W with screen/radar; a pocket-sized integrated panel is unlikely to keep it operating and recharge rapidly; an optional external foldable 20–60W panel should be evaluated, with operation schedule and actual draw.
All calculations omit unusual cold/heat behavior and extreme weather. For remote multi-day unattended deployment, include required energy reserve for consecutive poor-sun days and battery low-voltage recovery.

## Modular architecture decision: separate pack chargers and output combiner (2026-10-07)
User prefers **multiple inexpensive purpose-specific modules** over locating one all-in-one four-cartridge PCB. This is FEASIBLE IN PRINCIPLE, not a tested electrical design. Retain modular functional boundaries:
1. External USB-C / wall and optional solar PV interface (correct PD/input rating, solar MPPT or input dynamic power management, backfeed/current limiting); one managed external power distribution bus.
2. Regulated priority system power path (5V for the current display/radar configuration), capable of carrying full load from external source OR source-switched battery cartridges.
3. **Independent charger channel per removable four-cell cartridge**, or carefully switched *sequential* shared charger if strict slot-to-slot isolation and pack recognition are implemented. Concurrency is optional; charge current must fit remaining input budget after servicing the live load. Per-channel independent charger is easier to understand/test and monitors each pack separately. Option for removable external dock using a charger per socket, with connector and thermal monitoring, and no simultaneous connection to two chargers.
4. **Independent discharge path for each cartridge**, with properly selected pack protector/fuse, reverse current blocking and ideal-diode OR/power MUX with hot-insert/inrush protection, disconnection detection, thermal cutoffs. Output combiner prevents one cartridge back-feeding/recharging another. May combine after each pack's qualified converter, or OR single-cell pack voltage then use a common output boost converter; compare low-voltage drop/efficiency/current and failover. Choose ONE engineered distribution architecture in schematic; *never wire packs in raw direct parallel*.
5. A common 5V regulator/load bus with measured peak ratings, plus pack presence/SoC/voltage/current/temperature/fault sensors and user-visible status. Hardware controls safety independent of ESP32 firmware.
6. Removable cartridge is protected **1S4P** candidate (four matched 3.6/3.7V nominal cells in parallel) for simpler 1S CC/CV charging; the alternative **4S** needs 4S balancing+BMS and very different charging/output circuitry. Maintain one battery topology per cartridge, never assume two are interchangeable. At 3Ah x 4 = 12Ah nominal at ~3.6V (~43Wh) per cartridge; four such cartridges ~173Wh nominal. 1S4P bus changes roughly 3–4.2V in use; 5V regulator output stays 5V. Multiple packs add available energy/current capability subject to protection limits, but the load determines drawn current and OR paths do NOT automatically balance equal currents.
7. Per-pack protections both at **cartridge** and **device** level: appropriate cell pack protection BMS, pack-side short/cell polarity safety, pack fuse or current limiting, temperature sensor, physical recessed contacts; device per-slot input reverse block/inrush/hot-mating fault, current and voltage monitoring, external input surge/reverse protection, temperature/charge management, 5V output current limiting. In 1S4P rebuild user must NOT place a newly charged single 18650 into other partly discharged parallel cells: even offline, mismatched cells can dangerously equalize. Replace properly matched/reconditioned set or build more complex isolated cell channels.
8. One direct physical charger board per slot is NOT necessarily the same as a board suitable to simultaneously power an ESP32 and charge: for example TP4056 module lacks correct load-share/simultaneous power path by itself, even if it includes DW01 protection. BQ24074 single-cell module has load-share, but current/voltage must be assessed; 1.5A LOAD at <4.4V is not a regulated 5V output. BQ25798 is one independent pack charger and supports 1–4 **series** cells, solar input and power-path; it is an IC/evaluation board, not 4 different removable packs on one pin. Evaluate module availability/cost honestly; design may use independent chargers plus a separate common priority power path rather than relying on integrated charger load ports.
9. **Shared charger alternative:** one suitably rated 1S charger can sequentially connect to one recognized protected cartridge at a time via break-before-make switched charge circuit; not parallel multiple cartridge charging, and requires controller, hardware failsafes and pack-dependent charge start/stop. Trade lower PCB cost against much more control/logic and time required to charge 4 packs. Independent separate charger per slot is recommended for first build.
10. USB/solar power budget example: if ESP32/display/sensors consume 6W and two cartridges are each charging at 1A near 4.2V, battery charging alone requests ~8.4W plus charger losses; a 5V 2A adapter provides only 10W and cannot support it all. Must reserve headroom and limit/sequence chargers or use a properly negotiated higher-power supply and properly rated input converters. Solar panel label ratings are peaks, not guaranteed delivered power.
11. Test staged: stable 5V external source -> one protected pack & one charger -> second independently isolated pack and hot swap at measured peak load -> USB direct load plus independent concurrent or sequenced chargers -> solar dynamic load management -> scale to 4 bays, all packs/source combinations, status and fault diagnostics. Do not claim any assembly is safe until electrical verification of insertion, thermal limits, overcurrent, reboot prevention, charger interlocks.
Specific reference components: TI TPS2121 2-input, ~4A power MUX https://www.ti.com/product/TPS2121 ; TI LM66100 ideal diode, only 1.5A per IC (may be insufficient for a single high-load cartridge) https://www.ti.com/product/LM66100 ; TI BQ25798 https://www.ti.com/product/BQ25798 ; Adafruit BQ24074 https://www.adafruit.com/product/4755 . Use manufacturer docs for engineering ratings; no claim these form a plug-and-play full device.

## Cartridge charging convenience: USB-C + dock + installed charging (2026-10-07; PLANNED)
User confirms that each complete removable **four-18650 1S4P cartridge** should support **charging through an onboard USB-C input** AND **drop-in charging through a dock**, while retaining in-device charging through handheld/remote station external USB-C/wall/solar source. These are separate supported power entry points, not three chargers simultaneously shorted together.
- Cartridge includes one validated 1S charger matched to protected ~12Ah nominal 1S4P pack (for 3Ah cells) with compatible pack voltage/current/temperature sensing, plus proper BMS/pack fuse and thermal fault inhibition.
- The cartridge charger can receive a properly conditioned input from (a) its own USB-C receptacle, (b) a distinct protected/polarized recessed charging-contact pair when sitting in a powered desktop charger cradle, or (c) compatible charging contacts when installed in handheld/remote station. Select, multiplex or reverse-isolate charger *inputs* so simultaneous connection is defined and cannot backfeed USB or the dock. USB-C needs proper 5V sink configuration; requesting higher PD voltages needs an actual USB-C PD sink controller and voltage-rated charger.
- Device system power does not rely on charger being enabled: separate protected/disconnect-safe battery output connection per cartridge feeds isolated ideal diode/MUX paths and common 5V regulated rail. This is important for wall/solar direct load support and whole-cartridge hot swapping.
- Dock can be mechanically simple: supply rated power and contact safety to each inserted cartridge whose own internal charger regulates its cell pack. If dock charges multiple cartridges, limit total adapter power and protect each bay. Alternative architecture with charger per *dock/device bay* is also possible but would make stand-alone cartridge USB-C charging redundant with its onboard charger; avoid inadvertently wiring two active charging circuits into one cell pack.
- Verify USB-C or dock charger amp rating against 1S4P ~12Ah capacity: a 1A charger needs >12h in ideal conditions from fully depleted, and realistically longer during CV taper; charging speed requires proper higher-current charger, adequate cable/contact ratings, USB source power and heat management. USB-C connector alone doesn't imply USB Power Delivery or high-current charging.
- Pogo and/or other blind-mate docking contacts must be recessed/short-protected, self-aligning, rated for planned current and cycle life. Prevent live shorting if dropped or dirty; appropriate USB-C port mounting and dust cover for outdoor remote station use.
- Mechanical CAD: cartridge USB-C access, charging pads at insertion end, user removal latch, keyed rail. Keep room for service access to 18650 cells only after complete removal; don't swap individual parallel cells at differing charge levels.
- Test each path separately before simultaneous/overlap testing: standalone USB-C charged cartridge; desktop dock; installed station charge while operating; external USB and solar priority; loss/recovery with cartridge switchover. Log charge source, per-bay current, errors and case temperature.
